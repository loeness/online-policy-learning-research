# Baselines and related work

## Latest write-investigation controls

The new write-decision tests compare dynamics-only and reward-aware grouping, strict/relaxed benefit gates, episode-separated reference projection, local promotion, and retention of the same trial entirely in local parameters. Bounded caches add LRU/LFU/random replacement. An added G0 control uses all fresh rollouts for training and spends zero paid uniform-probe interactions; total training-plus-probe interactions match shared conditions, while updates, compute, and known per-bout dwell times do not. All-stream reward is used for that comparison.

Reference projection is inspired by A-GEM and conflict-projection methods; it is not a faithful published reproduction. The newer setup adds reward conflicts, detour shaping and two independently initialized pretrained foundations, so the older single-foundation scope below describes historical cohorts. No literature leaderboard or SOTA comparison is claimed. [Latest results and limits](WRITE_INVESTIGATION_REVIEW.md).

## Scope and comparison rule

This note maps the implemented A, B, and C1 controls to relevant published methods. It is not a leaderboard. This project uses a custom 9 × 9 navigation simulator with four actions, one fixed behavior-cloning foundation checkpoint, three deployment seeds per formal comparison, and project-specific online schedules. Literature scores from other task suites, training corpora, model sizes, budgets, or memory definitions cannot be ranked against these results.

The training algorithm is PPO through Stable-Baselines3. PPO alternates environment sampling with stochastic optimization of a clipped surrogate objective; the SB3 paper is a software implementation and reliability reference, not evidence that this custom benchmark has external validation ([Schulman et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347), bibliography key Schulman2017PPO; [Raffin et al., 2021](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-1364.html), Raffin2021SB3). The environment API is Gymnasium ([Towers et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17032), Towers2024Gymnasium).

| Compression follow-up | Nine representation arms, including compact dense, Top-K, action-contrast, activation-weighted pruning, columns, rank-2 SVD, distilled rank-2 SVD, and BF16. | These are separate representation and online-repair diagnostics on existing A/B checkpoints. They are not C1 methods, cross-task parent consolidation, or evidence of compute acceleration. See the [compression report](../outputs/compression_alternatives/结果解读.md). |

In the C1 report, the fixed tree's mean final success over six task variants is 100.00% at the low budget and 99.60% at the high budget; the corresponding flat means are 60.68% and 80.50%, and dictionary means are 94.95% and 93.94%. These are descriptive results from three deployment seeds per method and capacity, all sharing one foundation checkpoint. The structural family and region prior was known, the tree was fixed, and the formal main phases used oracle routing. These scores support a controlled result on this task construction; they do not establish state-of-the-art performance or superiority on a standard continual-RL benchmark.

Likewise, the analytic A/B controller is a real negative result for broad claims: on the matched A cohort it scored 6,329.81 mean cumulative reward versus 4,706.31 for the global PPO residual; on B it scored 11,875.88 versus 8,736.96 for history-routed PPO residuals. This controller directly exploits the known global-rotation structure using online transition evidence, with no policy-gradient updates. Its advantage does not imply that online parameter learning is generally unnecessary; it means the A/B task cannot demonstrate that neural parameter updates are necessary or superior to temporary history-based adaptation.

## Relevant methods that were not run

Cal-QL calibrates an offline-RL value initialization for online fine-tuning ([Nakamoto et al., 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/c44a04289beaf0a7d968a94066a1d696-Abstract-Conference.html), Nakamoto2023CalQL). This project initializes from behavior cloning and then uses on-policy PPO; it does not pretrain a critic with offline RL or implement these value-learning and replay procedures. They are important future comparisons, but their published scores are not comparable to the current custom runs. |
| In-context/meta-RL adaptation | PEARL infers probabilistic task context online ([Rakelly et al., 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/rakelly19a.html), Rakelly2019PEARL). AMAGO and AMAGO-2 use sequence models for long history, memory, and unlabeled multi-task adaptation ([Grigsby et al., 2024a](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/7204434dcb9383a1454dc1e97e58ea9c-Abstract-Conference.html), Grigsby2024AMAGO; [Grigsby et al., 2024b](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/9f40d2612d0b3b6f6c2a77da21b1067f-Abstract-Conference.html), Grigsby2024AMAGO2). | None were run. These methods spend capacity and training on context/history conditioning and meta-learning. | Replay and online multi-task transfer | CLEAR combines replay-based off-policy learning and behavioral cloning with on-policy learning to balance stability and plasticity ([Rolnick et al., 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/fa7cdfad1a5aaf8370ebeda47a1ff1c3-Abstract.html), Rolnick2019CLEAR). PG-ELLA transfers through a shared policy-gradient basis ([Bou Ammar et al., 2014](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/ammar14.html), BouAmmar2014PGELLA). Lifetime Policy Reuse keeps a fixed-size policy library and studies task capacity ([Bossens & Sobey, 2024](https://doi.org/10.3233/AIC-230040), Bossens2024LifetimePolicyReuse). | None were run. Replay methods retain data, which must count against any bounded deployment-state budget. PG-ELLA is a useful shared-basis comparison. Lifetime Policy Reuse makes policy count an explicit capacity choice, but its published task protocol and policy selector differ from this project. |
| Continual-RL evaluation and structure | Continual World is a robotic benchmark emphasizing forward transfer, forgetting, capacity, and compute ([Wołczyk et al., 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/ef8446f35513a8d6aa2308357a268a7e-Abstract.html), Wolczyk2021ContinualWorld). C1 is a fixed-structure RL mechanism study. They are not direct score comparisons because their tasks and learning protocols differ. |
| State-dependent model/history adaptation | The public preprint *What Must Generalist Agents Remember?* analyzes when an agent must retain memory to distinguish observation-identical situations with conflicting optimal actions ([Yamin et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18746), Yamin2026GeneralistMemory). | The result concerns memory requirements, not a method in this repository and not a proof that persistent neural-weight updates are required. | Bounded adaptivity theory | Wang et al. analyze batch updates and rare policy switches for linear-function-approximation episodic MDPs ([Wang et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02195), Wang2021Adaptivity). | This is relevant to how often an algorithm updates or switches policies under explicit linear-MDP assumptions. It does not directly bound neural parameter bytes, optimizer state, memory capacity, wall-clock time, or FLOPs in this implementation. |

### C1 supplement: these gaps have now been tested

The state-dependent history-only controls now exist in `outputs/c1_supplement/step1_models`: categorical window counts and Bayesian assignment models using actual observed transitions. They receive no task labels, switch hints or hidden maps. They have explicit visible-band, grid geometry and bijection assumptions; they are strong controls for this particular action-remapping task. Clearing their working window removes their online evidence. See [the supplemental report](../outputs/c1_supplement/结果解读.md).

The supplement also includes: tree versus exactly equivalent flat grouped addition; separately learned family dictionaries; individually validated flat/dictionary/tree settings; full shared actor/critic adaptation; a task-free empirical diagonal-Fisher EWC adaptation; and 128-item bounded PPO behavioral replay inspired by CLEAR. These EWC/replay controls are custom adaptations, not replications of the original published algorithms or SOTA. Full controls are outside the original adaptive caps and use a declared 393,216-byte complete-state cap. All negative validation candidates and formal failures of performance remain saved. A stronger full actor with small context heads and compact source-basis representations are separate predeclared C1 cohorts with fresh seeds, described in [C1_ALTERNATIVE_RESULTS.md](C1_ALTERNATIVE_RESULTS.md).

[EWC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796) supplies a parameter-importance penalty; [Progress & Compress](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/schwarz18a.html) supplies methodological context for separating acquisition from consolidation. Progress & Compress itself was not implemented. [CLEAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682) combines replay and behavioral cloning in its own off-policy learning setup; this PPO control implements only bounded policy-distribution replay. These distinctions prevent custom controls from being mislabeled as published SOTA reproductions.

The published sequence/meta-RL and offline-to-online methods above are also worthwhile candidates, but a faithful comparison would require their own training procedure and data exposure. Simply comparing their published benchmark scores with this project's six-task navigation scores would be misleading.

## Compression references are methodological context

The compression follow-up cites LoRA for low-rank trainable factors added to a frozen pretrained model ([Hu et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685), Hu2022LoRA), Eckart–Young for best rank-r approximation in Frobenius norm ([Eckart & Young, 1936](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02288367), Eckart1936LowRank), Wanda for activation-weighted pruning ([Sun et al., 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/14c856c7a41297804de4c4890e846b25-Abstract-Conference.html), Sun2024Wanda), and teacher-student compression ([Hinton et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531), Hinton2015Distillation).

The project's rank-2 SVD arm truncates a small action-residual matrix and continues updating its factors. It is not a replication of LoRA's Transformer adapters. The activation-weighted pruning arm is inspired by Wanda but adapts its score to these residual weights; it is not the paper's original LLM method. The distillation arm is a repair step for a compressed residual, not evidence for a general continual-consolidation method.

## Research agenda and software provenance

*Welcome to the Era of Experience* argues for agents learning from extended streams of grounded interaction ([Silver & Sutton, 2025](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf), Silver2025EraExperience). It is useful motivation, not a theorem or quantitative baseline. The project notes also mention an unpublished supervisor argument; this note does not include its private manuscript, quote its proof, or claim to have independently verified it.

The project adapts the experiment-loop idea in Andrej Karpathy's public [autoresearch repository](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch) (Karpathy2026Autoresearch). It did not run that repository's nanochat/GPT training setup. The [Scientific Agent Skills preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00065) (Kassis2026ScientificAgentSkills) is cited for procedural workflow context only. It reports no task-level evaluation and is not an algorithm-performance reference.

## Limits of current comparisons

The current evidence supports narrow statements about these controlled navigation tasks: persistent residuals can aid A/B revisits; a specialized analytic controller is stronger than the current A/B PPO adaptation; and a fixed tree can transfer online-learned residuals to held-out variants under the C1 structural prior. The experiments do not establish a win over published continual-RL, meta-RL, offline-to-online, or model-based methods. External rankings require those methods to be implemented on the same task suite with matched information, training data, interaction schedules, memory accounting, and tuning effort.

## Source-basis alternative: similarity and difference

[LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685) freezes pretrained weights and learns low-rank updates. [LoRA-XS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17604) trains a small middle matrix between frozen low-rank matrices derived from pretrained-weight SVD. [PG-ELLA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/ammar14.html) transfers policy-gradient knowledge through a shared basis. The C1 output mixer similarly places small trainable coefficients in a retained basis, so this parameterization alone is not a new general compression contribution.

Our diagnostic uses the centered four-action W0 head as a three-dimensional source output basis, visible-band coefficient matrices and optional shared family coefficients. Its coefficients are updated by real deployment reward and survive clearing the working window. It is not a Transformer LoRA-XS implementation, PG-ELLA's algorithm, or automatic knowledge-tree discovery. The source-output span is especially favorable for C1 command permutations; tasks requiring genuinely new features or decisions need an expanded or learned basis. The wider research question remains bounded persistent consolidation of online experience under unknown context boundaries.
